What is on the other side of the world?

The point on the exact opposite side of the Earth from you is your antipode. For most people it is sea: only about one-eighth of the world's land has land on the other side. Here is the other side of the world from 32 cities, worked out from the same map Look Up and Down uses, and how to see your own.

Updated 26 November 2026

See yours: point your phone down

What an antipode is

Your antipode is where a straight line from you through the centre of the Earth would come out: 12,742 km away, the Earth's full width, or about 20,000 km round the surface whichever way you go. To find it, swap your latitude between north and south and move your longitude 180° round the globe. Brisbane, at 27.47°S, 153.03°E, has its antipode at 27.47°N, 26.97°W, in the Atlantic Ocean.

See your own

Open Look Up and Down on your phone, tap Start, and point the phone straight down at your feet. The Earth turns to glass, and the place in the middle of the view is your antipode, seen from underneath, so the map is back to front. The card names it and gives the distance. On a computer, drag the view downwards. Each row of the table below also opens the page standing in that city.

Could you dig a hole to China?

Not from the United States. Every part of the lower 48 states has the Indian Ocean on the other side: a grid of 3,321 points across them, half a degree apart, all come out in the southern Indian Ocean, between Africa and Australia. From New York you would surface about 1,070 km off Australia; from Chicago, about 1,300 km from the nearest land, France's Amsterdam and Saint-Paul Islands.

To come out in China you would have to start in Argentina: Beijing, Shanghai and Hong Kong all have Argentina on the other side. From Santiago in Chile you would come out in China itself, and from Buenos Aires in the sea about 130 km off the Chinese coast.

The hole would be 12,742 km long, through the crust, the mantle, the liquid outer core and the solid inner core.

Land opposite land is rare

About 29% of the Earth's surface is land, but only about 13% of that land, one-eighth, has land on its antipode, by an even sample of 200,000 points over the globe on the same map. Some of the pairs in the table: Spain and New Zealand (Madrid and Auckland), Argentina and Chile with China, Colombia with Indonesia (Jakarta), Ecuador with Singapore, Peru with Cambodia (Lima), and Botswana with Hawaii (Honolulu).

Questions

What is my antipode?

The point on the exact opposite side of the Earth from you. Swap your latitude between north and south and move your longitude 180° round the globe, or open Look Up and Down and point your phone straight down.

Is the other side of the United States in China?

No. The whole of the lower 48 states is opposite the southern Indian Ocean, between Africa and Australia. China's antipodes are in Argentina and Chile.

What is opposite the United Kingdom?

The Pacific Ocean south-east of New Zealand. London's antipode is about 210 km from New Zealand's Antipodes Islands, and Dublin's about 310 km from Campbell Island.

How far is it to the other side of the world?

Straight through the Earth, 12,742 km, its width. Round the surface it is about 20,000 km, half the way round, whichever direction you go.
